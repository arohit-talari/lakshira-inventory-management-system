# Discovery & Requirements: Interview Framework

**Business Analyst Interview Guide**

This is the question framework used to run a combined stakeholder discovery and requirements-gathering session, stage 1 of the full [Lakshira project](../README.md) and the process behind [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md), not just its output.

The stakeholder's actual answers are not included here. They contain real financial figures, customer and supplier specifics, and other business-confidential detail that isn't appropriate to publish. What's shown is the methodology: how the conversation was structured to move from "how does this business actually work today" to "what does a system need to do about it," in a single session appropriate to a one-person engagement with a single decision-maker.

## How this was run

The session followed a conversation, not an interrogation format: starting with open, low-stakes questions to get the stakeholder talking naturally about her business, then following the threads she opened rather than sticking rigidly to a script. The most valuable answers came from going off-script, rarely from mechanically working down the list.

---

## Section 1: Current State
*Understanding how the business actually operates today*

1. Walk me through a typical week. What do you do Monday through Sunday, and roughly how much time does each activity take?
2. When you talk to a supplier, what information are you walking away with, and where does that information go after the call?
3. How do you decide how many of a particular saree to order? What would make you order more or fewer?
4. When a saree arrives, what happens to it, physically and administratively, before it's ready to sell?
5. How do you currently know what you have in stock at any given moment?
6. When a sale happens, walk me through exactly what you do from the moment a customer says yes to the moment the package leaves your hands.
7. What does your record of that sale look like? Can you show me an example row?
8. How do you handle a situation where a customer wants something you don't have?

## Section 2: Pain Points
*Where the friction lives*

9. What part of your week feels like the most wasted time or the most frustrating?
10. Have you ever made a decision that you later wished you had more information for? What was it?
11. Have you ever run out of a saree that was selling well and couldn't restock fast enough?
12. Have you ever been stuck with inventory that didn't move? How long has it been sitting and what did you do with it?
13. Is there anything you feel like you're doing manually that you wish just happened automatically?
14. When a customer asks you a question about a product, is the answer always at your fingertips or do you sometimes have to go look for it?
15. Have you ever lost track of an order, a payment, or a customer request?

## Section 3: Customers
*Understanding who she's selling to*

16. Who is your typical customer? How would you describe her?
17. Do you have customers who buy repeatedly? How often do they come back?
18. Do you notice any patterns in what different types of customers buy: occasion, price range, style?
19. How do customers usually find you: Instagram, word of mouth, WhatsApp, something else?
20. Have you ever had a customer you lost that you wish you hadn't? What happened?
21. What does a customer usually need from you before they commit to buying: a call, more photos, specific information?
22. Do your local customers behave differently from customers who order from out of state or internationally?

## Section 4: Suppliers
*Understanding her sourcing relationships*

23. How many suppliers do you currently work with?
24. Are all suppliers equal, or do you trust some more than others for quality or reliability?
25. How do you track what you've ordered from which supplier and when?
26. Have you ever had a supplier issue, wrong item, delay, quality problem? How did you handle it?
27. Do any suppliers offer you exclusivity on certain designs, or is everything available to other distributors too?
28. How far in advance do you typically need to plan an order before it arrives ready to sell?

## Section 5: Financials
*Understanding the money*

29. Do you know your total revenue for last year? This year to date?
30. Do you know which category generates the most profit for you, not just the most revenue?
31. How do you decide the price of a saree? Walk me through an example from cost to final price.
32. Have you ever changed a price after listing, marked something down or up? What triggered that?
33. Do you have a sense of how much money is currently tied up in unsold inventory?
34. Are there months that are significantly better or worse than others for sales?

## Section 6: Goals and Vision
*Where she wants to go*

35. What does success look like for this business in two years? In five?
36. Is there a revenue number or a customer count that would feel like a real milestone to you?
37. Do you want to eventually have a proper website and storefront, or do you prefer the personal, direct model you have now?
38. Are there parts of the business you'd want to hand off to someone else as it grows, and parts you'd always want to keep doing yourself?
39. What's the one thing holding you back from growing faster right now?
40. If you had a dashboard that showed you exactly one thing every morning to help you make better decisions, what would you want it to show?

## Section 7: Data and Tools
*Understanding what exists*

41. Can you show me every spreadsheet or document you currently use to run the business?
42. How often do you update them: in real time, daily, weekly, whenever you remember?
43. Is there anything you track in your head or in WhatsApp that isn't written down anywhere?
44. Have you ever tried any tools or apps to manage the business and stopped using them? Why?
45. How comfortable are you learning a new tool if it genuinely saved you time?

---

*This framework, run once as a single combined session, directly informed the data model, the 10 operations scoped into the IMS, and the analytics layer described in [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md). Every one of those decisions traces back to a specific answer given here.*
